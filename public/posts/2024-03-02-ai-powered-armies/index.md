# AI-Powered Armies

## Introduction

If you think that AI will never be used in the military because it is immoral, I am sorry to disappoint you: **it already happened.** AI-powered drones, aim-assistance for weapons, guided artillery, and target recognition have been around for a while, and more weapons will have one form or another of machine learning implemented.

Even if we are not soldiers, I think it is essential for us to understand what role AI will play — not only to get a sense of how future conflicts might look, but also to be prepared to advocate against the immoral use of such weapons and limit their destructive power. However, as we will see, **AI's main impact might reside in the field of logistics.**

In a world where technological advances can be both an existential threat and a certainty of power, no government will want to be left behind in the race to get tools that can prove more efficient than traditional firepower.

---

## The New Nuclear Weapon

While the two concepts might seem unrelated (a massive explosive vs. a software model replicating human intelligence), the deployment of AI in military defense is on the same magnitude, if not higher, as the atomic bomb. 

The atomic bomb's power is not measured solely in kilotons, but in its ability to intimidate. A big explosion is easy for people to grasp, and its potential to destroy mankind was rapidly understood by the collective intelligence of nations. Even if a nation used small tactical nukes, it would be considered unacceptable by the international community.

The same might not hold for Artificial Intelligence. AI is an extremely difficult concept to grasp, even for experts. It will be even more difficult to be understood by the general public, and therefore public outrage against AI might not have a single clear target. Humanoid robots walking on a field are already a deep-rooted fear thanks to movies and novels, but AI will take far subtle forms than that.

Would people suspect that the drones they see on war footage are not controlled by a human operator? Probably not. How about a map or a schedule? Would people be afraid of an optimized map? Most certainly not.

I am talking about the field of **logistics** — the biblical David against sheer firepower where large AI models will play an essential role in future conflicts. Logistics win wars. A maxim in defense says:

> *“Amateurs talk strategy, professionals talk logistics.”*

AI will be at the heart of military logistics by planning routes, optimal material allocations, food distribution, deployment timelines, weather adaptations, and supply chain resiliency.

---

## Logistics Shaped by AI

According to the Cambridge Dictionary:

> *“Logistics is the careful organization of a complicated activity so that it happens in a successful and effective way.”*

Logistics is a critical aspect in every major branch of our economy. The recent global pandemic showed everyone that supply chains have grown increasingly complex. Our ability to manually plan and maintain these systems is limited because every minute, every item, and every schedule shift can be critical in avoiding massive losses.

War is no different. Soldiers need food, ammunition, clothing, spare parts, and building materials. In fact, in a regular army, only a fraction of personnel fight directly on the front line. As of 2022, the global Military Logistics market was estimated at **USD 397 billion**, a figure expected to grow past **USD 550 billion** by 2028.

With the rise of deep convolutional networks and fine-tuned models, logistics will be changed forever with the introduction of AI. However, AI needs huge amounts of data to perform well. War is by definition a one-time, highly dynamic action involving immense destruction in unpredictable environments. Therefore, the immediate scope of military AI will focus on areas similar to civilian applications: **logistics, predictive maintenance, tactical simulations, and rapid reporting.**

---

## The 7 Principles of Military Logistics

The United States MCWP 3–40 Marine Corps Logistics defines 7 core principles of logistics:

1. **Responsiveness:** Providing required support when and where it is needed.
2. **Simplicity:** Elimination of needless complexity to foster unity and efficiency.
3. **Flexibility:** Adapting to changing situations and requirements.
4. **Economy:** Using the minimum amount of resources required to accomplish an objective.
5. **Attainability:** The existence of sufficient supplies and distribution capabilities to initiate an operation at acceptable risk.
6. **Sustainability:** Maintaining the necessary level and duration of logistics to accomplish an objective.
7. **Survivability:** The capacity to prevail despite adversary attacks.

---

### How AI Enhances the 7 Principles

#### 1. Responsiveness & Scenarios
Simulating thousands of scenarios — whether battles, natural disasters, or supply bottlenecks — using reinforcement learning could help logisticians create robust supply chain models, discover hidden vulnerabilities, and optimize resource deployment beforehand.

#### 2. Communication & Generative Assist
Communication can benefit immensely from live translation AI, active noise-canceling signal assistants, and speech-to-text reporting tools in the field. Generative AI will also simplify complex briefings and technical manuals into clear visual diagrams.

#### 3. Economy & Resource Allocation
Handling vast amounts of inventory data while accounting for safety reserves is a massive task for humans. AI agents supervised by human operators can predict exact material needs, transport hours, and energy consumption in seconds.

#### 4. Survivability & Mobile Modeling
We can expect the emergence of **Mobile Modeling Units** — mobile edge computers equipped with GPUs that run local AI models on the field even when internet connectivity is severed.

#### 5. Battle Planning & Field Scoping
Seconds decide a battle. AI can process drone footage, map optimal terrain routes, identify potential hiding places or vantage points, and compute safe supply paths for squadrons in real time.

#### 6. Field 3D Printing & AI Calibration
3D printers on the front lines enable on-demand fabrication of replacement parts. However, harsh field conditions (vibrations, mud, dust) disrupt printer calibration. AI active-calibration algorithms can adjust motor steps and print parameters in real time to guarantee precision 3D printing under extreme conditions.

#### 7. Camp & Infrastructure Planning
Military camps are massive logistic challenges. Bad layout planning can expose fuel and ammunition depots to hazards. City-building simulations powered by AI allow commanders to generate safe, optimized camp layouts in minutes.

---

## Conclusion

AI has already transformed defense systems. Autonomous target tracking and missile defense systems operate at speeds no human operator can match. However, outside of aerial drones, the primary revolution of AI in the military will happen **behind the lines in logistics, planning, and signal processing.**

AI should be restricted to increasing precision and logistics efficiency to minimize harm. Using fully autonomous machines to decide human lives should be universally condemned. As we enter this new era, understanding these technologies is our responsibility to advocate for ethical boundaries and human safety.
